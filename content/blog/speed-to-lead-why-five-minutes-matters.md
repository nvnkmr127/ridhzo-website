# Speed-to-Lead: Why the First 5 Minutes Decide the Deal

Every inbound lead has a shelf life measured in minutes. The team that replies first — with the right message — almost always wins the deal. Yet most sales teams still take hours to respond, quietly burning the ad budget that generated the lead in the first place.

## The 5-minute cliff

Study after study of inbound sales finds the same pattern: the odds of qualifying a lead drop off a cliff after the first few minutes.

| Time to first response | Relative likelihood of connecting |
| :--- | :--- |
| Under 5 minutes | Baseline (highest) |
| 30 minutes | Sharply lower |
| 1 hour+ | A fraction of the 5-minute rate |
| Next day | Mostly cold |

The reason is simple: at the moment someone submits a form, they are **actively thinking about the problem you solve.** Wait an hour and they've moved on, filled out three competitor forms, or lost the urgency entirely.

## Why teams still respond slowly

- **Leads scatter across channels.** Meta Lead Ads, Google forms, website forms, WhatsApp, email — nobody is watching all of them at once.
- **No instant alert.** By the time a lead surfaces in an inbox or a spreadsheet, the window has closed.
- **Manual first contact.** Saving a number, opening WhatsApp, typing a greeting — three minutes per lead adds up fast.

![Sales team following up with leads on phone and laptop](/figma/617a0.png)

## How to actually hit the 5-minute window

1. **Unify capture.** Every lead, from every source, lands in one place the instant it's submitted.
2. **Alert the right rep instantly.** A vibrating push notification beats an email that gets read at lunch.
3. **Make first contact one tap.** Pre-filled WhatsApp templates turn a 3-minute task into a 3-second one.
4. **Route automatically.** Round-robin assignment means no lead waits for someone to "claim" it.
5. **Measure response time.** What gets measured gets fixed — track first-response SLA per rep.
